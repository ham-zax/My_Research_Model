# Experiment 001 receipt-time revision candidate

Status: **prepared, not selected for primary events, labels or model fitting** (2026-09-24). Policy `E001-receipt-time-candidate-1`; proposed experiment `e001-v1.3-candidate`, feature schema `E001-live-features-receipt-candidate-1`, label schema `E001-label-v4-candidate`. The current `e001-v1.2` candidate and its strict replay remain the default. This revision follows the owner's choice to prepare a receipt-time policy after the paired BTC audit; it is not a freeze.

## Measurement contract

At integer UTC second `t`, use the latest *received* Binance and Bybit BTCUSDT spot top-of-book states in capture order. Each top must be valid, initialized and received no later than `t`; its last top change must be at most five seconds old by application receipt time. The spot reference is the equal average of the two midquotes. A missing venue or stale/invalid top makes the grid price unavailable. The five-second value comes from the already approved quote rule, but **receipt age is a changed definition**: it does not by itself bound time since the venue generated the quote.

Trade and liquidation-size flow windows use `(t-w, t]` on application receipt time. Bybit book changes, one-second observed price-level persistence, ticker values, lagged states and the 1,800-second pre-trigger scale use the same receipt timeline. Original venue timestamps remain in raw records and feature provenance; no event is moved before its capture receipt. The feature formulas and missing economics are inherited from the current live diagnostic builder. Liquidation execution notional, order-level persistence and cancellation/execution attribution remain unavailable.

If this policy is eventually selected, **triggers and both labels must be rebuilt** from its spot grid under a new label schema. The 1%/300-second crossing, two-hour lockout, 15-second decision delay, first-passage barriers and horizons are proposed to stay the same. No receipt-time event or label extraction has been performed, and old source-time labels cannot be joined to this candidate's features.

## Current fail-closed quality gate

`--timing-candidate receipt_v1` emits diagnostic rows with `timing_quality.state`, ordered reasons and evidence fields. All rows have `source_freshness_certified: false`, `primary_eligible: false` and `model_ready: false`. The states are `warming` (required observed history or feed initialization missing), `unknown` (operational checks pass but independent timing proof is missing), and `quarantined` (local clock step or gross source-clock conflict). There is no path to `qualified` in this version because the required evidence is not present. These states describe why a candidate is blocked; a numeric diagnostic feature is not an eligible model value.

Operational checks require all three BTC connections, initialized Binance spot/Bybit spot/Bybit linear books, a Bybit ticker snapshot, both fresh receipt-time spot quotes, subscribed perpetual flow with no more than 30 seconds of source silence, and a **minimum 1,815-second observed span** since capture start or the last feed disruption. That span covers the full 1,800-second pre-trigger scale plus the 15-second decision delay; it does not prove every required grid state is present. A connection error/disconnect or a collector `event_loop_delay` restarts the span. Existing book/flow builders independently null unsupported windows, sequence failures, partial 25-bps bands and unavailable scales. Local wall/monotonic `clock_step` quarantines the rest of the run; recovery requires a new, independently verified epoch and initialized history and is not automatic in this candidate. The report's `clock_valid_rows` for this mode counts only rows without a detected local wall-clock step; it is not UTC certification.

As an extra **rejection-only** guard, any WebSocket envelope timestamp more than 100 ms ahead of its application receipt quarantines the run. The 100 ms is the collector's existing local-step detection magnitude, reused as a conservative gross-conflict cutoff. It is not an estimated exchange clock offset, a permissible packet delay, or proof that leads below 100 ms are harmless. A healthy feed with a larger legitimate clock offset could be excluded. The original 3.3-second error sample trips this guard; the observed 0.331859-ms disagreement in the later run does not.

Two required checks currently have no passing evidence:

1. **Independent UTC clock health.** The capture's wall and monotonic clocks detect steps but cannot prove absolute UTC alignment. The previously observed Windows/WSL repair is useful operational evidence, but it was not recorded as a causal, independently trusted attestation in each raw capture.
2. **WebSocket-role source-delay bound.** Public HTTP server-time probes have a different timestamp domain from Bybit orderbook `ts`, trade/liquidation `T`, ticker `ts`, and Binance depth `E`. Neither the capture nor reviewed exchange documentation establishes a numerical offset/drift bound from those WebSocket roles to the local clock. Therefore a received quote or flow packet may be old even when its receipt is recent. The candidate has `source_delay_bound_ns: null`; it must not infer one from HTTP round-trip time, ping/pong, a lag median, or the five-second receipt cap.

Before primary selection, record a contemporaneous independent UTC health signal and a supported per-role WebSocket source-time relation with expiry/drift and a prespecified maximum admissible delay. Apply the upper age bound to every required source, including quiet channels and book snapshots; reject unknown, expired or delayed sources. Specify recovery after a clock step or feed interruption, then run a fresh bounded capture. If no defensible source-delay evidence is obtainable, report the receipt-time experiment as observationally unqualified rather than fitting a model from apparently fresh packets.
